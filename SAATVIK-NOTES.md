## 1. Simple explanation (for website visitors)

**Saatvik** (Sanskrit *sattva* — goodness, clarity, balance) describes food that is pure, fresh, and nourishing — food that supports a calm mind and healthy body, not agitation or dullness.

At **Cafe Gopala**, saatvik is not a marketing label on a few dishes. **Every item on our menu** is:

- Pure **vegetarian** — no meat, fish, or eggs  
- **No onion, no garlic** — none, in any dish  
- **No mushroom** — none, in any dish  
- Cooked fresh, with care — the way we would serve family and friends  

We follow the saatvik standard taught in the **Bhagavad Gita** and honoured in **Vaishnava** tradition — the same path of **Srila Prabhupada** that inspired Cafe Gopala from the beginning.

**One line for the hero or menu intro:**  
*Fully saatvik — pure vegetarian, no onion, no garlic, no mushroom — in every dish.*

## 2. The three gunas (qualities of nature)

In **Samkhya** and **yogic** philosophy, everything in nature — including food — is influenced by three *gunas* (qualities):

| Guna | Quality | Effect on mind (tradition) | Food examples (general tradition) |
|------|---------|----------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Sattva** | Goodness, clarity, balance | Calm, clear, steady — supports meditation and devotion | Fresh grains, milk, ghee, fruits, most vegetables, legumes, mild spices |
| **Rajas** | Passion, activity, restlessness | Stimulation, desire, agitation | Very hot/spicy/salty/sour foods; **onion & garlic** (pungent, heating) |
| **Tamas** | Ignorance, inertia, dullness | Heaviness, confusion, lethargy | Stale food, alcohol, overcooked leftovers; **mushrooms** (fungi, grow in darkness/decay) |

**Key idea for Cafe Gopala:** We choose ingredients in the **mode of goodness** (*sattva*) so the food supports wellbeing — for guests who come to eat peacefully, and for those who follow a devotional way of life.

## 3. What Hindu scriptures say

### Chandogya Upanishad — food and the mind

**7.26.2** (Sanskrit):

> *Āhāra-śuddhau sattva-śuddhiḥ, sattva-śuddhau dhruvā smṛtiḥ, smṛti-lambhe sarva-granthīnāṃ vipramokṣaḥ.*

**Meaning (widely cited translation):**  
When food is pure, the inner being becomes pure. When the inner being is pure, memory becomes steady. When memory is steady, the knots of the heart are released.

**Also 6.5.1** — food nourishes the body at three levels: the grossest part becomes waste, the middle becomes flesh, the **finest part becomes the mind**. So what we eat literally shapes how we think and feel.

**Website use:** A gentle, non-technical line — *Ancient wisdom teaches that pure food nourishes not only the body but the mind.*

Sources: [Chandogya 7.26.2 — Anuśīlana](https://anusilana.in/ahara-suddhau-sattva-suddhau/), [Wisdom Lib 6.5.1](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/chandogya-upanishad-english/d/doc239278.html)

### Bhagavad Gita — Chapter 17, verses 8–10

Krishna classifies food by its effect on life, virtue, strength, and happiness.

**17.8 — Saatvik food** (*sāttvika*):

> *Āyuḥ-sattva-balārogya-sukha-prīti-vivardhanāḥ — rasyāḥ snigdhāḥ sthirā hṛidyā āhārāḥ sāttvika-priyāḥ*

**Translation:** Foods that increase life, virtue, strength, health, happiness, and satisfaction — juicy, nourishing, wholesome, and pleasing to the heart — are dear to those in the mode of goodness.

Examples in commentaries: grains, pulses, fruits, vegetables, milk, ghee.

**17.9 — Rajasic food:** Excessively bitter, sour, salty, hot, pungent, dry, burning — causes pain and disease.

**17.10 — Tamasic food:** Stale, tasteless, putrid, leftover, impure.

**Website use:** *The Bhagavad Gita describes saatvik food as fresh, nourishing, and life-giving — food that brings strength, health, and joy.*

Sources: [BG 17.8 — Holy Bhagavad Gita](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/17/verse/8/), [BG 17.7–10 — bhagavad-gita.org](https://bhagavad-gita.org/Gita/verse-17-07.html)

**Note for accuracy:** Verses 8–10 classify food mainly by **quality** (freshness, taste, effect). The **specific** exclusion of onion, garlic, and mushroom comes from **Ayurveda** and **Vaishnava prasadam** tradition applied to those verses — not by name in the Gita itself. On the website, it is honest to say: *“Following the saatvik standard taught in the Gita and honoured in Vaishnava tradition…”*

### Ayurveda (Charaka & Sushruta Samhita — classical summary)

- Onion and garlic: **pungent** (*katu rasa*), **heating** (*ushna virya*), classified as **rajasic–tamasic**
- Used as **medicine** in Ayurveda, but not recommended as daily food for those seeking mental clarity and spiritual life
- Mushrooms: classified as **tamasic** — fungi, no chlorophyll, associated with darkness and decay

Source summaries: [Sattvic Gyaan — onion & garlic](https://sattvicgyaan.com/why-do-hindus-avoid-onion-and-garlic/), [Wikipedia — Sattvic diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sattvic_diet)

## 4. Why no onion, garlic, or mushroom? (Vaishnava / ISKCON tradition)

This is the tradition **Srila Prabhupada** established for ISKCON — aligned with Cafe Gopala’s kitchen rules.

### Onion & garlic (and the allium family)

- Botanically **alliums** — same family as leeks, chives, shallots  
- Classified **rajasic** (stimulating) and **tamasic** (dulling) — they excite the senses and disturb calm meditation  
- **Cannot be offered to Krishna** as *prasadam* (sanctified food) in Vaishnava worship  
- Ayurvedic authority Dr. Robert E. Svoboda (often quoted in ISKCON sources): *“Garlic and onions are both rajasic and tamasic, and are forbidden to yogis because they root the consciousness more firmly in the body.”*

**Important nuance for honest copy:** Onion and garlic have **medicinal** uses in Ayurveda. The avoidance is about **daily food for spiritual life**, not claiming they are “poison.”

Sources: [ISKCON Desire Tree — Why no garlic and onions](https://food.iskcondesiretree.com/why-no-garlic-and-onions/), [ISKCON — Culture / Food](https://content.iskcon.org/culture/food/index.html)

### Mushroom

- **Fungi**, not plants — grow in darkness, often on decaying matter  
- Classified **tamasic** — associated with inertia and dullness  
- Also **not offered** in Vaishnava prasadam alongside onion and garlic  

Sources: [Lotus Ministry — Why Vaishnavas avoid garlic, onions & mushrooms](https://www.lotus-ministry.org/post/why-vaisnavas-avoid-garlic-onions-mushrooms), [The Bhakti House — foods avoided in prasadam](https://thebhaktihouse.org/why-onion-and-garlic-are-not-used-in-prasadam-and-other-foods-traditionally-avoided/)

### Other foods traditionally avoided in prasadam (for reference — confirm Cafe Gopala menu)

Srila Prabhupada’s guidelines for offering to Krishna also exclude:

- Meat, fish, eggs  
- Alcohol, coffee, caffeinated tea  
- Red lentils (*masur dal*) — in strict ISKCON prasadam kitchens  

**Action:** When writing website copy, only claim what the **POS menu actually excludes**. Confirmed: no onion, garlic, mushroom, meat, fish, eggs.

## 6. What saatvik food includes (Cafe Gopala friendly list)

Good to show visitors what they **can** enjoy — positive framing:

- **Grains & breads:** rice, wheat, naan, roti, paratha, biryani (veg), dosa/idli when South menu returns  
- **Legumes:** dal, chole, rajma, moong  
- **Dairy:** milk, paneer, ghee, butter, lassi, chai  
- **Vegetables & fruits:** seasonal, fresh  
- **Mild spices:** cumin, coriander, turmeric, cardamom, cinnamon — flavour without rajasic excess  
- **Sweets:** gulab jamun, jalebi, halwa (when saatvik-prepared)  

**Flavour without onion/garlic:** Traditional saatvik cooking uses asafoetida (*hing*), ginger, green chilli (in moderation), tomatoes, and spice blends prepared without alliums — Cafe Gopala’s kitchen already works this way.
