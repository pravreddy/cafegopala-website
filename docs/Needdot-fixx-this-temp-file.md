The Scriptural FoundationIn the Bhagavad Gita (Chapter 17, Verse 8), Lord Krishna describes Sattvic food:"Foods that promote longevity, purity, strength, health, happiness, and satisfaction—which are juicy, nourishing, wholesome, and pleasing to the heart—are dear to those in the mode of goodness."  According to Ayurveda and Vedic philosophy, all foods carry one of three fundamental energies (Gunas):  Sattva (Purity & Goodness): Light, fresh, and calming foods that foster mental clarity, vitality, and devotion.  Rajas (Passion & Agitation): Overly spicy, pungent, or salty foods that overstimulate the mind and nervous system.  Tamas (Ignorance & Lethargy): Stale, processed, or heavy foods that dull consciousness and lower physical vitality.  Why We Avoid Onion, Garlic, and Mushrooms While onion and garlic are widely used in modern cooking, Vedic scriptures and Ayurvedic science classify them as Rajasic and Tamasic.  Onion & Garlic: They agitate the central nervous system, stir up passion, and disturb the inner calm required for meditation and spiritual focus.  Mushrooms: Because they grow in darkness and belong to the fungus family, they carry Tamasic energy (inertia and darkness) and are strictly avoided in pure Sattvic preparation.  Instead, we enhance natural flavors using aromatic spices like cumin, ginger, coriander, turmeric, cardamom, and pure Hing (asafoetida) cooked in fresh ghee or cold-pressed oils. Here are authentic quotes by Srila Prabhupada specifically focusing on Sattvic food, the mode of goodness, and the spiritual power of pure food, along with their exact scriptural and lecture references:

1. On Purifying the Mind & Senses

"If you eat sattvic food, in the mode of goodness, then your mind becomes calm and quiet. A peaceful mind can understand higher spiritual values, whereas rajasic and tamasic foods agitate the mind and senses."

2. On Health, Longevity, and Consciousness

"Foodstuffs in the mode of goodness—such as milk products, grains, sugar, butter, fruits, and vegetables—increase the duration of life, purify one's existence, and give strength, health, happiness, and satisfaction."

Reference: Bhagavad-gita As It Is, Purport 17.8

3. On the True Purpose of Food

"The purpose of food is to increase the duration of life, purify the mind, and aid physical strength. This is its only purpose. In ancient times, great sages crafted diets of foods that enhance health and longevity, such as milk products, sugar, rice, wheat, fruits, and vegetables."

Reference: Bhagavad-gita As It Is, Purport 17.8

Reference: Srimad-Bhagavatam, Purport 4.29.53

5. On Elevating Human Consciousness Through Eating

"By accepting sattvic prasadam, a person's fine brain tissues are developed, enabling them to understand subtle spiritual truths. Unregulated eating of passion and ignorance destroys these delicate mental capacities."

Reference: Light of the Bhagavata, Purport 29
